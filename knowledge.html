01 GRAMMAR

Pronouns & possessives

Choose the form by its job in the sentence — not simply by translating the Russian word.

Before a noun: my book. Without a noun: This book is mine. There is no commonly used independent possessive form of it.

A noun follows
This is her bag.

her tells us who owns the bag. We still need the noun bag.

No noun follows
This bag is hers.

hers already means “her bag”, so the noun is not repeated.

Don't confuse its and it's

its = belonging to it: The cat is washing its paws.

it's = it is or it has: It's cold. / It's been a long day.

Possessive its never has an apostrophe.

Giving something to someone vs doing something for someone

to often introduces the receiver: She gave the tickets to us. = She gave us the tickets.

for often introduces the person who benefits: He bought a present for me. = He bought me a present.

These two word orders work with certain verbs; don't assume you can move the person before the thing with every verb.

02 VOCABULARY

Talking about names

English uses several different words for the parts of a person's name. Here is what each one means.

first nameThe personal name given to you at birth
middle nameA name between your first name and surname
surname / last nameYour family name
full nameYour first name, any middle names and your surname
initialsThe first letters of your names
nicknameAn informal or alternative name people use for you
maiden nameA woman's surname before marriage, if it changes
married nameA surname someone uses after marriage

Useful patterns

be called

Her name is Katherine, but she's called Kate.

for short

My name is Alexander — Alex for short.

be named after

He was named after his grandfather.

pseudonym

A writer may publish a book under a different name, or pseudonym.
